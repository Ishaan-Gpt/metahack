## 1. Tailored Labs, Bolt World's Largest Hackathon (the closest match to Moovi)

- **What it is:** an AI video timeline editor. You describe edits in plain words ("remove silences", "find the best hook") and it cuts dead air and filler, syncs audio and picks the best moments. https://bolt.new/blog/2025-bolt-hackathon-winners , https://www.producthunt.com/products/tailored-labs/launches/tailored-labs-v2
- **Problem and market:** creators with hours of raw footage who hate editing (YouTubers, course makers). Product Hunt pitch: "AI video editor that does 80% of the work for you." Bolt's post claims it turns 8 hours of editing into 4 minutes (marketing claim, not measured by me).
- **Proof in production:** it launched on Product Hunt on 2 July 2025, before the hackathon results, so it was a live product, not a demo. Product Hunt shows 132 followers; no user or revenue numbers found (UNVERIFIED).
- **Scale it beat:** 130,000+ registered builders, $1M+ total prizes. Submission count not stated.
- **Judging:** impact or viral potential, idea quality, technical implementation including novel use of Bolt, design and polish (https://bolt.new/blog/hackathon-prizes-global-glory-regional-highlights-bonus-wins). Judges were investors, founders and engineers; no quotes published.
- **Pitch line:** "AI video editor that does 80% of the work for you."
- **Demo video / first 10 seconds:** UNVERIFIED (no video found).
- **Why it stood out (my reading, partly inference):** a single clear job, one sentence to explain, a result any viewer can feel (raw footage to a rough cut), already shipped. Caution: it is the same space as Moovi, so judges have seen "AI video editor" win once; the angle must differ.

## 3. CrossBeam, Anthropic "Built with Opus 4.6"

- **Pitch:** "Everyone thinks California has a housing crisis. We don't. We have a permit crisis." (Mike Brown, a personal injury lawyer who says he never wrote or read the code.)
- **Problem and market:** California housing permits have a 90%+ first-submission rejection rate, about six months of delay, costing homeowners about $30,000. Buena Park must permit 8,900+ units by 2029 but permitted about 120 in 2024.

- **Judges' words:** none published. Prize for 1st not stated; 500 invited participants, one week, $500 credits each.
- **Demo first 10 seconds:** UNVERIFIED.
- **Why it stood out:** a number-backed pain, a named city with a real deadline, and a non-engineer builder story.

## 5. Guardian, Meta Llama Impact Hackathon London (Meta's own audience)

- **What:** an AI triage assistant for NHS A&E departments on Llama 3.2, with a clinical agent (Atlas) and multilingual patient communication. Targets long waits and resource allocation.
- **Context:** 56 teams, 200+ developers, $50,000 shared by the top three, six weeks of mentorship, Groq 1,000+ tokens/s, Nebius H100 per team. Runners-up: Gripmind (Llama Vision controls a robotic arm), Pharmallama (on-device medication check). Meta pushes Llama Impact Grants up to $500K globally.
- **Production proof / judge words / demo:** none published (UNVERIFIED).
- **Why it mattered:** Meta rewarded public-good use of open Llama, on-device and open-source angles, not generic tools. https://about.fb.com/news/2024/11/metas-llama-impact-hackathon-pioneering-ai-solutions-for-public-good/

## Transferable principles

1. Say the problem as one number-backed sentence a stranger repeats (90% rejected, six months, $30,000).
2. A named buyer or a pilot beats a feature list. "Three faculties plan pilots" beat "we built X".
3. Show it already running: a product launched before the deadline, or a measured result (74% to 81%, 20 minutes).
4. Own one kind of value. Google split prizes by impact, creativity, usefulness; pick one and be the best at it.
5. The builder's own lived edge (lawyer, cardiologist, carpenter's son) makes the story credible.
6. Use the sponsor's tool where it is load-bearing, and say how. Bolt scored "novel use of Bolt"; Anthropic winners named parallel agents and Managed Agents.
7. Plain words win; the winning pitches are one sentence long.
8. Judges here are investors and founders, so show a path from demo to business.
9. Scale of entry is huge (130,000+ registrations); polish and a clear first moment are what remain.

## What this means for a video-agent startup entering the Meta hackathon

Tailored Labs shows a video editor can win a big-money hackathon, so the category is judged as real, but it also means you must not be "another AI video editor": pick one sharp promise, name who is hurt today with a number, and show a finished film made by Moovi from a plain request in the first ten seconds, ideally already live with real outputs. Make Meta AI load-bearing and say exactly where (which Llama model does the director's planning, scripting or captions, and what that cost or speed gives you), because 25% of your score is tooling and Meta's own past winners were public-good, open or on-device uses of Llama. Put one measured claim on screen (seconds from brief to film, cost per film, a blind comparison score) and one real user or pilot. I could not verify any winner's demo video, so treat the structure advice as inference from what judges praised, not a copy of a known demo, and confirm the Meta rubric and the $250k line on the official rules page before you build your pitch around them.
