## The WHY (the thought process, as transferable principles)
1. **Every phrase is a beat with its own "look state".** The edit never stays the same for more than about 2 s, yet it uses one take of one person sitting still. The variety comes from the editor, not the footage: shot size (wide, close-up, medium), background treatment (real, desaturated, black void, B&W), text layout and colour all change at phrase ends.
2. **The look of a beat follows what the line means.** Alarm or a statistic gets red, a black void and huge type ("PRESENCE", "%94"). The cosy mechanism "organic warms the audience" gets warm colours. The conclusion goes black-and-white and calm, and the colour returning on "make the ad spend work harder" is the payoff. Colour is a meaning system tied to the brand colour (red), not decoration.
3. **Text shows the stressed words, not every word.** Bold caps for the key word (HAPPENS, PAID, WARMS, ADS, AUDIENCE), small script for the helper words (what, to, the, convert). It builds word by word in sync with the voice, so the eye reads at the speed of the voice. It sits in the empty space beside the face, never on it, and sometimes **behind** the person.
4. **Contrast of scale drives rhythm.** Huge words (PRESENCE, %94) against tiny one-word subtitles at the end. Loud beats are followed by quiet ones.
5. **The person is separated from the world.** A rim line around the hair, a dimmed background, a black void, text behind the head: all need a subject mask every frame. This is what makes cheap footage look like a produced ad.
6. **Transitions are rare, different and motivated**: a light burn on "cold", a light-line wipe into the statistic, a particle dissolve out of "touchpoints", a white flash into B&W. Most changes are plain cuts; no transition repeats.
7. **Grade and brand frame the whole thing**: contrast, deep blacks, warm skin, a bottom fade to black where the logo lives, a logo end card. The before has none of this.
8. **Sound**: the caption claims "detailed sound design" (whooshes, hits, risers are typical). **Not measured from the audio yet.**
9. **The voice stays calm** (2.8 words/s). The energy comes from the edit, not from a fast read.

## The engine this implies (what Moovi would have to do)
1. **Hear**: transcript with word times; split into phrases (beats).
2. **Understand** (Muse Spark): give each beat a role (hook question, problem, statistic, mechanism, contrast, payoff, brand) and pick its stressed words and the meaning colour.
3. **See** (SAM 3.1): a person mask on every frame, plus the face box, so the engine can crop by shot size, dim/replace the background, draw a rim line and put text behind or beside the face.
4. **Cast** (library + rules): role → look state (shot size, background treatment, text layout recipe, colour from the brand palette, transition in), under constraints: no face covered, no look or transition twice in a row, loud then quiet, about one change every 1.2-2 s.
5. **Render**: kinetic text in sync with word times, grade, transitions, sound events on text pops and cuts, logo and end card.
6. **Check**: face safe, safe area, no empty frame, contrast, rhythm, then show before/after side by side.

## Rating
- **This reel as an edit: 8.5/10** (my judgment). Strengths: meaning-led colour, varied looks from one static take, word-synced text that never covers the face. Weak spots: some text blocks are crowded ("ADS convert THE warm AUDIENCE" uses four type styles), and the long end card (about 3.5 s of logo, about 15% of the reel).
- **As the hackathon idea ("give a before, get this after"): 8/10 as an idea, and the first prize depends on execution.**
  - For: the before/after split is understood in 3 seconds, which suits the 25% demo score; SAM 3.1 is the heart of the look (masks every frame), so Meta use is clearly load-bearing; Spark does the reading of meaning; the market is real (every creator, coach and brand).
  - Against: AI auto-editing is a crowded field (Captions, Submagic, Opus Clip; an AI video editor, Tailored Labs, won Bolt's $100k grand prize). Judges will have seen "auto captions". It only wins if the after looks like a hired pro made it, on a clip the judge brings.
