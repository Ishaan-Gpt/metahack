## edit-ba2: Tahrima, "3 reasons your edits look nothing like Instagram"
https://www.instagram.com/p/DaFaIEGIFvx/ (posted 2026-06-27, 8,045 likes, 388 comments). 45.5 s, 30 fps, 166 words (3.6 words/s).
**Essence:** an editor's portfolio piece where the talk itself is a tip list. The viewer (a creator) sees the edit prove the tips.
The "Before" is shown **in black-and-white** beside the colour after, which exaggerates the gap on purpose.

What is on screen:
- **Ground:** the speaker sits on a saturated violet colour field (the real wall graded, or replaced). For the negative line
  "you're gonna get lost and your edit is not gonna look that good" the ground **turns deep red**: colour = meaning, again.
- **Captions:** only on some phrases, centred on the chest **behind the microphone** (the mic is in front of the words, so
  the person and mic are masked). One hero word is big and glowing ("look", "think", "animations", "effects", "just",
  "boring"), the rest of the phrase tiny above and below it. Long stretches (about 6-8 s at a time) have **no text at all**.
- **Punch-in close-up** with the face pushed to one side and a neon sign-style word beside it ("nothing like what i see on
  instagram" in cyan and magenta, the Instagram word in its own colours).
- **Chapter cards** for "Number one/two/three": the picture goes to a dark navy void, a huge ghost numeral fades up, a
  glowing diamond icon lands on it with the label ("lack of direction", "bad design", "poor post production"). Same card
  three times: it is the video's structure, so repetition is a feature here.
- **Diamond badges with "?"** pop around the speaker on "it's probably one of these three things".
- **Frame-in-frame:** on "know exactly what you want your scene to look like" the shot shrinks into a glowing outlined
  frame and **3D eyeballs** float around it (looking = "look like").
- **List builder:** "vignettes, blurs, chromatic aberration, noise, posterize" stack one by one as glowing outlined pills
  over the dimmed shot, each on its word.
- Grade: contrasty, the violet pushed, deep shadows. Sound: not measured.

Why it works: the tips are **shown by the edit itself** (talks about effects while showing them); the structure (1, 2, 3)
is a visual system the viewer can track; long quiet stretches make the hero moments land.
Rating: 8/10 (clean and on-message; the A-roll stretches without any change for 6-8 s are slow for a Short).

## edit-ba3: Daniel, "$10 edit vs $80 edit" (productivity tip)
https://www.instagram.com/p/DXjyhHEkwOV/ (posted 2026-04-25, 2,856 likes, 133 comments). 17.9 s, 30 fps, 58 words.
Caption (the editor's own why): "The footage wasn't good. But the video still works. Because editing isn't about adding
effects. It's about solving problems."
**Essence:** the raw footage is dark and dull; the edit replaces most of it with clean product UI that acts out every claim.

What is on screen:
- **A-roll is a layer, not the canvas.** The talking head is on screen for only about a third of the time. It shrinks to a
  rounded card, then a circle, then a mini avatar above a checklist, then comes back full-frame.
- **A white, Apple-clean canvas** with soft shadows; one warm accent (orange/gold).
- **Every claim becomes a UI object:** "10x my results" = a dark chart card ("Your Result $120,000", line drawing up,
  "10x"); "overnight" = the chart zooms in with a pill label; "planning my day" = a calendar emoji chip; "if it's not on my
  calendar it's not going to happen" = a checklist with red X ticks; "90% of the time" = a clock on a warm gradient with
  "THE TIME" in orange; "downloading Google Calendar" = an App Store search row, a cursor clicks "Download"; "scheduling
  your day" = calendar event pills stacking; "notification on your phone" = an iOS notification sliding in over the
  footage, then a close-up of it.
- **Captions:** first line "one of the biggest PRODUCTIVITY HACK" in white/gold over the dark footage; later most words
  live inside the UI, not as subtitles.
- **Camera:** smooth zooms into UI elements, blur-through transitions between scenes, the shot itself pushed and blurred
  when the UI takes over.

Why it works: weak footage is hidden behind strong, readable product visuals; each sentence has one concrete picture;
the viewer learns the tip from the pictures even with the sound off.
Rating: 8.5/10 (premium, clear; the opening caption is small for the hook).

## edit-ba4: Mahdi, interview clip (MrBeast on making videos)
https://www.instagram.com/p/DdM89KRMPBJ/ (posted 2026-09-12, 9,019 likes, **6,337 comments**: a comment-for-DM call).
23.8 s, 30 fps, 99 words (4.1 words/s). Footage is a third party's interview (MrBeast); used here only for analysis.
**Essence:** interview footage made to feel like a premium documentary: warm, tactile, with a 3D desk world that acts out
the process he describes.

What is on screen:
- **Captions:** small white phrase subtitles at chest height; the hero word of each phrase **glows yellow and big**
  ("on videos", "in advance" in orange, "filming", "Editing", "from start to finish", "100 grand a video", "1.5 million in
  video"). Some words reveal with a **letter scramble** (glyphs flicker into the word).
- **A 3D desk world** for the process: a sunlit beige desk with window-light shadows, leaves, a coffee cup, a pencil. "We
  brainstorm what they are" = a 3D brain, a light bulb switching on, sticky notes "concept, story, style"; "1-2 months" =
  a storyboard sheet clipped, a dashed arrow, a video player card with an UPLOAD tag; "three to four months working on
  the idea" = the bulb fills the frame, glowing. The camera pans and pushes across the desk.
- **Transitions:** blur-through into and out of the desk world, a warm light-leak flash before the money line.
- **Punch-in** to a close-up for the money numbers, and **dollar bills falling behind his head** (masked: the bills pass
  behind the person).
- Grade: warm-teal background, warm skin, a dark fall-off at the bottom where the captions sit.

Why it works: numbers and time spans are abstract, so they get physical objects; the desk world gives the clip a
"documentary" production value no interview footage has; the money moment is the payoff, so it gets the closest shot
and the only particles.
Rating: 8.5/10 (the desk world is beautiful; the subtitles are small and low-contrast in places).
