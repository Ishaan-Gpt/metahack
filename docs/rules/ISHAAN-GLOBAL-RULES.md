# Ishaan's global rules (every project)

- **No silent defaults.** Never keep a colour, font, ease, layout, caption style, sound, preset, template or saved
  rule just because it came by default. Ask "can something be better?", write at least two alternatives, keep the
  default only if it wins, and say why.
- **Colour: never the AI-default palette.** No blue/indigo + orange, cream/beige + terracotta, navy + ice blue,
  teal + orange, purple-pink gradients, neon on black, Tailwind slate neutrals or pure #000/#FFF. Pick a rare,
  premium palette derived from the subject (a pigment, a material, a place, the footage itself), build OKLCH tint
  ramps per hue (one dominant, one support, one accent of 10% at most, neutrals tinted with the dominant), and design
  contrast from the ramps. Full method: `~/.claude/skills/ishaan-motion/references/palette.md`.
- A brand's own colours are kept as its mark; the world around them still follows the rule above.
- **Every correction Ishaan gives becomes a rule, without being asked.** When he says something is off (colour,
  type, layout, motion, sound, the storyboard format, how to present work), write it the same turn into: the
  style- or type-specific reference it belongs to, AND the common Ishaan skills it touches
  (`~/.claude/skills/ishaan-motion`, `~/.claude/skills/ishaan-ui`), plus this file when it applies everywhere.
  Bump the skill's VERSION and CHANGELOG, and mirror the skill into any repo that keeps a copy
  (Moovi: `.claude/skills/ishaan-motion/`). Tell the other running sessions in the same project to do the same.
- **Show work as something he can play**, not only stills, whenever it moves (a live preview link that plays with
  sound), served with byte-range support so video can seek.
- **Text never covers a face, and on Shorts it stays inside the safe area (y 230-1480, x 70-905)** (captions, titles, stickers; Ishaan 2026-10-25): place it beside or below the face with a decent margin, using a face detector; a difference/negative highlight takes its colour from the background behind it.
- **Films are fast, varied and directed** (Ishaan, 2026-10-21): pacing for short attention spans (about 3.2-3.8 words a second, 0.05-0.25 s gaps, a visible change every 1.2-2 s, hook inside 2 s); no transition twice in a row and none more than about twice a film, entrances varied by meaning; one voice only, one continuous fast read (about +25-30% rate), never per-line pitch or rate shifts (they sound like different voices). Details: `ishaan-motion` showreel-youtube-automation.md. **Launch/brand films are the exception** (Ishaan, 2026-10-23): soft, slow and premium, keynote pacing with a calm read and breaths kept (ishaan-motion launch.md).
- **Never a black or empty frame** in any video: a beat whose clip or picture failed takes a broader search, a still,
  or another clip at a new moment. And a promo film is about the brand and understood by second 3, never a topic film.
- **Re-read the skill's newest rules right before building anything Ishaan will see** (2026-10-21, after a 4/10 and
  2/10 look board that broke a rule written that morning): parallel sessions add rules during the day.
- **Films are made 100% in code** (Ishaan, 2026-10-21): looks, frames and motion (three.js / Remotion / canvas), no
  generated images and never ask him for images. A code look still never ships as primitives: measured 3D craft
  (ishaan-motion taste.md) and the blind ratchet first.
- **A reference is the WHY, never the WHAT** (2026-10-20). Extract the thought process and taste as transferable
  principles, never reuse its elements (sfx, captions, transitions, colours) as a template. The premise and title formula are WHAT too (Ishaan, 2026-10-23): a new superpower in Ifology's "What IF You Could X" shape is a copy. Reference analysis and a
  film each target 2-3 minutes, in shots not frames; the goal is 10-15 min of finished video daily. See
  `~/.claude/skills/ishaan-motion/references/why-not-what.md`.
- **Write in author's English, never AI English, in plain easy words a half-watching viewer follows on first listen** (Ishaan, 2026-10-22, 2026-10-25): three levels, AI English < human English < author's English. Cut signposting ("here's...", "remember that"), the repeated "it's not X, it's Y" reveal, tidy triplets, explained jokes and stock similes; a narrator with a point of view, concrete nouns and verbs, varied rhythm. Details: the `author-english` skill (load it before any script): flowing, joined sentences with the feeling inside, never stacked short ones (Ishaan, 2026-10-25).
- **A reference's character is rebuilt as that character, never worse** (Ishaan, 2026-10-26): "my version of the character" means the reference's own character (same silhouette and face), redone in 3D and improved; it must beat or match the reference in a blind side-by-side, and nothing reaches Ishaan under 8/10.
- **Simple, nailed** (Ishaan, 2026-10-27, 4/10 on "Regrow Humanity"): never attempt complex things that can't be nailed (crowds, vast scale shots, complex body actions, many sets). Like the reference channels, pick little complication (few characters, simple movements and actions, few places) and nail each 10/10 with story, lighting and captions.
- **Bright, pastel, soothing** (Ishaan, 2026-10-27): story films default to bright themed worlds with pastel colours full of life, soothing animation (slow eases, gentle camera), and a nailed camera, light, contrast and grade. "Simple but impactful >>> complex but error-y."
- **Hooks are storytelling, and his reference sheets are the look** (Ishaan, 2026-10-28): a cold open meets the hero and the stakes first in plain words, and shows a rule before any gag that depends on it. When he gives reference sheets (character, world, props, graphics) for his own show, rebuild them in code as the spec and drop any palette or character derived before them.
- **Games and contests are told the MrBeast way** (Ishaan, 2026-10-28, 2/10 on a parade of strangers): one rule plus a visible threat, proven by the first try; three or four featured tries by characters the viewer already knows, each a 15-30 s story (set-up, the try at life speed, result, reaction, the count aloud); mass exits narrated in one line; a calm read and compression in the narration; cuts stay fast (MrBeast averages 1.5-1.7 s) but cover one moment from many angles, never a new person per cut. Extract a reference's why from the videos themselves (transcripts, shot lengths), not from articles. Details: ishaan-motion lessons.md.
- **Emotion and suspense first, never easy losses** (Ishaan, 2026-10-29): viewers must feel for the characters; each featured character has a stake in their own words, every loss is a fight with a visible cost, the first loss is its own event, and a segment climbs to a climax decided by a character's choice. Fewer, heavier exits. Details: ishaan-motion lessons.md.
- **A long form after a Short is the next chapter, never a rerun** (Ishaan, 2026-10-31): no line or beat of the Short is repeated, and the long form must look better than its Short, never worse. Judge it blind against the Short before it reaches him. Details: ishaan-motion lessons.md.
- **Every film passes the speed gate** (speed lab, 2026-11-02): before building and before exporting, run the Moovi repo's `docs/agents/speed/preflight.mjs check <film>` and log stages with `tlog.mjs`; one session holds the GPU at a time (`preflight.mjs gpu take/free`); judge each scene as it is built (one fix round) on `grab.mjs` sheets of frames seeked from the live page, never on an MP4. **One export per film, at the very end, only after Ishaan has watched the live preview and said export** (Ishaan, 2026-11-03: "why so many exports"); it uses `export.mjs` (every chunk/tab starts with a 1 s pre-roll so splits never pop). Why and numbers: `docs/agents/speed/PLAN.md`; Ishaan's PC steps: `CHECKLIST-PC.md`. **A low-disk FAIL is yours to fix, never his** (Ishaan, 2026-11-03): check the app's storage report and the biggest files yourself, send stale ones (old installers, superseded film drafts, stale worktrees) to the Recycle Bin, and tell him what went.
- **Essence first** (Ishaan, 2026-11-03, "u are getting the essence wrong in itself"): before planning from any reference, write in one line what it IS for its viewer (what they came for, what is on screen) and check every beat against it. A how-to film shows the genre's famous channels' own clips as the target and makes ours beside them, never an invented story of ours (ishaan-motion why-not-what.md). **And the form, at the shot level** (Ishaan, 2026-11-03, 0/10 on a designed-card explainer): list what is on screen in the reference every 2 s from its frames, and use the same kind of picture; a how-to or build-in-public film shows the real work (real pages, prompts, files, outputs, footage) zoomed to the spoken line, never cards and charts standing in for it. **And the utility** (Ishaan, 2026-11-03, "very bad... no utility"): a how-to's spine is the steps the viewer can do after watching (the exact prompt or command, readable long enough to copy, the real output, the result); the maker's journey and scores only season it. **Watch every live preview in real time in a browser before sending it** (assets preloaded behind a loading gate, never a blank player, no stutter, calm camera, readable text). **A YouTube long form is for a stranger, not a report** (Ishaan, 2026-11-03): moving picture most of the time, text a small minority; a how-to's own demo must beat the hits it is shown beside; study the newest niche breakouts (last days, views over subscribers) from the videos, then build to the retention graph: reveal first, the problem, structured steps, a reason to stay promised early and paid last.
- **The medium follows the reference** (Ishaan, 2026-11-03): when the reference is 2D that feels 3D (layered planes, light, depth), build 2D with depth, never real 3D by habit; it takes less time and finishes higher (ishaan-motion look.md, "Medium").
- **Use a skill to its max, scored** (Ishaan, 2026-10-31, 4/10 on Ghostwriting): never build with part of an Ishaan skill; score the work line by line against the whole skill (ishaan-ui: the gate at the top of references/checklist.md) and show nothing under 8/10, with the scores.
- **Rigs only get better** (Ishaan, 2026-11-03, 5/10 on the Bhagat Singh Short): a new character starts from the best rig ever built (list every rig, name the best, beat or match it blind), never the nearest one; no separate kit day (Ishaan, 2026-11-03: "I don't have that much time"): the character is built inside the film and judged in its shots; a historical hero keeps his icon; shots are pre-vized as stills before animating (ishaan-motion lessons.md, checklist.md "Character").
